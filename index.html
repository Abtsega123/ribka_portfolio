Research & Experience

Master's Thesis Research — The George Washington University Sept 2025 – May 2026
Washington, DC
  • Built and trained a 177-node CA3-inspired spiking neural network in Python, using NumPy for numerical simulation, to study how a high-dimensional attractor system reconstructs structure from partial, noisy input — a deep learning approach to representation learning in biological neural systems.
  • Formulated a composite training objective (completion, separation, precision, recall error) and optimized coupled network parameters with derivative-free search, iterating on training dynamics and architecture design across parameter sweeps.
  • Achieved 91% peak reconstruction accuracy and zero recall error at 75% partial-input coverage, with separation error remaining statistically stable across cue levels.
